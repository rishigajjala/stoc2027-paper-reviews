HOW TO READ THIS COLLECTION

Merit and correctness are separate questions.

This is an independent, AI-assisted reading recommendation, not a STOC programme-committee decision. Scores summarize judgments made from the linked manuscript; they are not measurements of objective scientific worth. The catalogue contains submissions, not confirmed acceptances.

The merit score

Each criterion is rated from 1 to 5. Significance contributes 45%, novelty 25%, technical depth 15%, and exposition 15%. A score of 3 means a substantial specialist contribution; 5 is reserved for exceptional advances.

Author identity, institutional prestige, and AI-assistance disclosures do not determine scores. Merit scores assess the claimed contribution under its stated assumptions. They do not certify correctness; the separate proof status determines eligibility.

The correctness requirement

Main argument checked: the recorded central argument was traced with no unresolved substantive gap found, subject to any explicitly documented corrections and qualifications. This is a review judgment, not a formal correctness certificate.

Partial check · large proof: specific core steps were inspected, but the proof is too extensive for complete verification here. The review lists both checked and remaining work.

Papers with a demonstrated fatal flaw or an unresolved substantive concern are ineligible for the shortlist until that concern is resolved.

Desk-reject recommendations

Reserved for concrete manuscript-grounded reasons such as a false main claim, an essential missing argument, a fundamental model mismatch, or no identifiable theoretical contribution. Every recommendation must give evidence.

A modest result or difficult exposition alone is not a desk-reject reason. An unreviewed manuscript has no score.

Coverage and limitations

The PDF links identify the source and relevant pages. Novelty comparisons identify whether previous results were independently checked. Exactly 400 papers (24.0% of the 1,665 submissions) are selected. Merit score comes first; ties prefer a checked main argument, then higher review confidence, then submission number. The final number tie-break is administrative, not a claim of scientific superiority. Closely scored papers should be read as a group, not as precisely ordered scientific merit.
